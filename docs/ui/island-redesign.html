FridayEars · UI design · 12 Nov 2026

The island should show up every time, on the screen you're looking at

Today's island picks a screen once, flips between states on racing timers, and can freeze or go silent without telling you. This redesign keeps the notch, Right ⌥ and Wispr, and rebuilds everything underneath so the island is always correct, calm and readable.

What's wrong now

Why it glitches or never shows up

Found in tools/FridayEars/main.swift and your logs. The app logs nothing about the UI itself, so these come from the code, your display setup, and 62 launches since 6 Nov.

  • It picks a screen once, at launch.Your EK220Q monitor sits above the laptop. If a window on the monitor had focus when Friday started, the island lives on the monitor for the whole session. Unplugging, sleep or a resolution change never moves it, and it can end up off-screen.
    Open on the screen with your pointer every time the island appears. Re-place it on plug, unplug and wake.
  • Show and hide race each other.The window is ordered out and back in, driven by five separate delays (0.45, 0.6, 0.9, 1.5 and 2.2 s). A show that lands inside a hide's delay can be lost, and short gaps between sentences close and reopen it.
    The window never leaves the screen. Hidden means transparent and click-through. One function decides what's shown.
  • Polling every 0.3 s, answers out of order.A new /status request starts every 0.3 s even when the last hasn't returned. Each carries 30 alerts, every session and presence. A slow answer that arrives late puts back an older state.
    fridayd pushes a small UI state with a sequence number. The app drops anything older than what it has shown.
  • Two flags can freeze it.While listening or waiting is set, all updates are ignored. If the Right ⌥ release is missed, the island stays on "Listening" and never changes again.
    A watchdog checks whether the key is physically down and cancels a stale listen.
  • If fridayd dies, the UI goes quiet.Startup gives up for good after 150 s, and nothing restarts fridayd or the gateway if they crash. The island just never appears.
    A red "restarting" island, automatic restarts, and no startup cutoff.
  • It can get in the way of clicks over your tabs.While shown, a 520 × 142 window sits over Brave's tab strip, and nothing limits its clicks to the black shape. Only the transparency of its pixels decides what passes through.
    The window is sized to the island's shape, so only the black part is clickable.

Opens three ways

Click the island, click the menu bar icon, or double-tap Right ⌥. Esc or a click outside closes it. It opens under the island, on the same screen.

Needs you, first

Permission prompts and held alerts sit at the top. "Play" speaks the held alerts now.

Now

The current turn: your words, each step with its time, and the answer as it streams. Today's panel only has this part.

Masters

Each master with what it's doing and how long. Click one to open that session in VS Code. Watching these is Friday's main job, and today the UI doesn't show them at all.

Earlier

The last 50 turns and spoken alerts, so you can read what Friday said while you weren't listening. Click a row to expand it.

Type to Friday

For when you can't talk out loud. Typing or Wispr into this box goes to the same thread as voice.

Build plan

In dependency order, all of it

  1. fridayd: one UI state and a stream

    A /ui state (island, turn, permissions, held alerts, masters, threads, quiet) with a sequence number, pushed on /ui/stream. History of the last 50 turns and alerts. Quiet mode in the speaker. /status stays for scripts.

  2. App core

    Stream client with reconnect, the single state function, and watchdogs for the key, the mic and the stream. The supervisor restarts fridayd and the gateway, with no startup cutoff.

  3. Window manager

    The island window follows the pointer's screen at each open, is notch-attached or a pill, never ordered out, click-through when hidden, and sized to the island. Re-placed on display change and wake.

  4. Island views

    The ten states above, with real mic level from a meter tap and word-by-word captions.

  5. Panel and menu bar

    Sections, type box, thread chips, masters (opening the session in VS Code), Esc and double-tap. A menu bar icon that shows state: amber when you're needed, red when offline.

  6. Break it on purpose

    Unplug and replug the monitor mid-sentence, sleep and wake, kill fridayd mid-turn, drop a key release, run full-screen VS Code, and answer a real permission prompt by voice. Check app CPU at idle stays under 1%.
